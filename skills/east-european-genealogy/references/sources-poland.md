# Polish sources and Austrian Galicia

The records of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth (to 1795) and of Austrian Galicia
(1772-1918) are split between Polish archives and the Lviv archive (TsDIAL). A Galician
village of today's Ukraine can have its Roman Catholic books in Poland and its Greek
Catholic books in Lviv. Routes below were checked in October 2026.

## Kinds of record

| Record | Period | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Parish books (metryki), Latin forms | from about 1784 in Galicia, earlier in places | births, marriages, deaths by house number; grandparents from about 1825 |
| Status animarum, Easter confession lists | 18th century | households by name, often with ages |
| Grod and land court books (księgi grodzkie i ziemskie) | 15th to 18th century | deeds, sales, dowries, debts, inventories of the szlachta; family links stated in the acts |
| Tax registers: podymne, pogłówne, ofiara | 17th-18th century | heads of households, sometimes the whole household |
| Szlachta legitimation files (Galicia, from 1782) | late 18th to 19th century | pedigree proofs with copies of older acts |
| Estate inventories (inwentarze dóbr) | 17th-19th century | inhabitants of a village by household |
| Church visitations | 18th century | clergy, parish lists, sometimes the parishioners |

The court books are the main source before parish books begin. An act names the parties,
their fathers, spouses and estates, and a run of acts across decades is a pedigree.

## Catalogues and scans

**szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl.** The Polish state archives' catalogue and scan viewer. Each unit
page says whether it has scans («This file does not have scans yet»); that line describes
only the online copy. The unit itself may well exist. Fonds of the Przemyśl archive include the
Greek Catholic bishopric with 18th-century soul lists and visitations. Many units are
microfilmed and readable only in the reading room.

**Geneteka** (`geneteka.genealodzy.pl`). The volunteer index of Polish parish books, by
region, parish and year. Strong for the 19th century. A hit gives the parish, year and act
number; read the act itself on the scan. A search by surname with the place filter set to
a whole province is a fast way to find which parishes a surname lives in.

**Metryki.genealodzy.pl** (scans of parish books) and the regional societies' indexes
cover parishes that Geneteka has not reached.

**repcyfr.pl** (Repozytorium Cyfrowe Poloników, a dLibra library). Scans of single acts from
the Przemyśl grod books (TsDIAL f. 13, op. 1) from the late 16th to the late 17th century, each
act an object with its date, kind, persons named (normalized to Polish) and shelfmark. It
also holds the Brańsk grod books of Podlasie, which a common surname will mix in.

- Search: `https://repcyfr.pl/dlibra/results?q=<word>&action=SimpleSearchAction&type=-6`.
  Diacritics matter: one surname returned nothing as «Kamiński» and 26 acts as «Kaminski».
  Search without diacritics first. The results page answers a browser and may refuse a
  plain script request (503 in October 2026); the OAI-PMH endpoint answers both.
- A PDF of an act: `https://repcyfr.pl/Content/<edition>/download/`.
- The full metadata is harvestable through OAI-PMH
  (`/dlibra/oai-pmh-repository.xml`, `ListRecords`, `oai_dc`). Harvest it once and search
  the local copy; date the snapshot, since acts are still being added.

**AGAD** (Archiwum Główne Akt Dawnych, Warsaw). Crown records (Metryka Koronna), magnate
family archives (the Radziwiłł archive among them), the records of the Four-Year Sejm, and
civil-religious books of former Galician parishes. Much is scanned on szukajwarchiwach.

**Digital libraries for printed sources**: Polona (the National Library), DBC and the
Lower Silesian and Wielkopolska digital libraries (dLibra network, searched together through
FBC), Google Books and HathiTrust. Use them for armorials (Niesiecki, Boniecki, Uruski),
published court records (Akta grodzkie i ziemskie, AGZ) and regional histories.

## Reading and searching

- Read `reading-manuscripts.md`, section on Galician Latin forms, before the parish books.
- Full-text search in these libraries does not normalize Polish declension. Search
  Kamińscy, Kamińskich, Kamińska and the Latin Kaminscius separately, and repeat without
  diacritics.
- Armorials assign coats of arms to families, sometimes late and to one branch only. A coat
  of arms is no filter for identity.
- Galician szlachta often carried a family byname (przydomek) that separates branches of one
  surname in one village (Terlecki Petrylowicz, Terlecki Szymkowicz). The court books and
  legitimation files use it; the later parish forms drop it.
- Published genealogies of szlachta families are reconstructions. Read their footnotes, open
  the acts, and keep the acts' version when the two disagree.
