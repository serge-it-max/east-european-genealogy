# Reading 18th and 19th century manuscripts

Rules for reading Cyrillic cursive (skoropis) and Latin parish forms of the region. Each
one was learned from a misreading that went into notes as a finding and had to be taken
back.

## The main rule: a reference word on the same sheet

Check a doubtful letter against a word on the same page where that letter is certain. Never
check it against a general idea of the letter's shape: hands differ, and the same scribe
writes the same letter differently in different years.

Words that almost always give a reference: the common given names (Stefan, Feodor, Grigory, Ioann,
Vasily) and the stock words of the form (maiden, faithful, godparents); household headings of the same list.

For a doubtful capital, look at the neighbouring households. In one 1766 soul list the
households Lazorev and Danich stood next to each other and settled capital Л against
capital Д for the whole sheet.

## Confirmation bias is the main error

When you look for one surname, the eye builds it out of similar outlines. Treat any reading
that matches the sought root as unconfirmed until the reference check is done. If the user
reads the scans, show the crop and the competing reading, and let the user decide. A theory
about letter shapes from one reader, human or agent, is no evidence; the user's reading of
the crop is.

Never build a conclusion on your own doubtful readings. A claim "these two spellings
alternate for one man" needs crops reviewed first.

## Ukrainian and Russian cursive, 18th century

| Pair | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|
| т and м | т has a stroke on top; м is the same shape without it. In fast writing the stroke looks like a stray flourish. |
| д and г | д has a loop going down, like Latin g; г has no loop. |
| н and м | н is two strokes, м is three. Check against the name Ioann on the same sheet. |
| Д and Л (capitals) | look alike; settle on a neighbouring household. |
| ѣ (yat) and в | yat often looks like в inside a word. Before reading в in a surname, compare with a certain yat on the sheet. |
| з + ѣ and дв / гв | з with a descender reads as д or г, and the yat after it as в. A root read as Lodv- or Logv- may be Lozě-. |
| Г and З (capitals) | check the parish's known surnames before inventing a new one. |

Grammar traps: the Church Slavonic verb bysha ("were"): "vospriemniki bysha …" means "the godparents
were …", and it is no name.

## Dates in Church Slavonic numerals

Years in vyaz numerals: ѯ (60) and о (70) are easy to confuse. Never date a page from the
vyaz year alone. Anchor it on a dated dean's note, a later Arabic year in the margin
("Rok 1768"), and check that the run of pages goes forward in time.

A header year can be a scribe's slip: a page headed 1788 that sits before the dean's note
of April 1788 belongs to 1787.

## A Latin signature settles the Cyrillic one

Priests in the Uniate and early Orthodox books of right-bank Ukraine often sign in both
scripts. When the same hand writes «Łoziński» in some entries and a doubtful Cyrillic form
in others, read the Cyrillic from the Latin.

## Galician Latin forms (from about 1784)

Greek Catholic and Roman Catholic books of Galicia are in Latin, in printed column forms.

**The marriage form from the late 1830s:**

    N. filius/filia <father> <estate> in <village>
      et <mother> nata de patre <maternal grandfather>
      atq̃ matre <maternal grandmother> de <her family>

- `atq̃` is atque, "and". At a line break it is easily read as a separate Polish word.
- This form gives the **mother's** parents and never the father's. Looking for the paternal
  grandfather in it is pointless; it is the design of the form.
- It does not write family bynames (przydomki). Match on the parents' names, the house
  numbers and the grandmothers, never on the surname alone.
- A Ruthenian name form (Wasyl) among uniformly Latin ones (Basilius) falls outside the
  scribe's norm. Flag it: it often goes with a note about estate or rite.

**Grandparents appear late.** Baptism records start naming grandparents (avus, avia, with
maiden names) from about 1825, often only from the 1830s. When an early baptism names only
the parents, search forward: the baptisms of the grandchildren and the marriages of the
children.

**House numbers.** Record every house number (sub N° domus) where the family appears, and
read the deaths from those houses. Ages at death give birth years even where names are
missing.

## Folio numbers

The folio number in an archive inventory and the number written on the page can differ by
a constant offset (one case had about 227 between them). A page "with the wrong number" is
often the same book under its old numbering. Check the offset at two known points before
relying on it, and never mix the two systems in one note.

## Dictation shorthand

When the user dictates from a scan, abbreviations are the user's, and typos are expected.
Keep the dictated line verbatim in the raw notes and interpret it in the parser or the
data, never by editing the raw text. Ask once for any abbreviation you do not know and
keep a glossary.
