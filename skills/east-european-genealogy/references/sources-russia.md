# Russian provincial sources

For the Russian Empire's central and eastern provinces the family lists are the revision
tales and confession lists; metric books fill the gaps between them. Before the first
revision (1719) the sources are the records of service people. Routes below were checked
in October 2026.

## Revision tales (revizskie skazki)

Ten revisions, each a list of the taxable male population by household, later with women:

| Revision | Year | Revision | Year |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1719 (to 1727) | 6 | 1811 |
| 2 | 1744 (to 1747) | 7 | 1815-1817 |
| 3 | 1762 (to 1767) | 8 | 1833-1835 |
| 4 | 1782 | 9 | 1850-1851 |
| 5 | 1795 | 10 | 1857-1858 |

- From the 4th revision on, each tale gives the household at the previous revision with what
  happened since (died, recruited, moved, "in absence"). Two tales side by side give the
  family across sixteen to twenty years.
- Estates have separate tales in the same revision: state peasants, odnodvortsy, townsmen
  (meshchane), serfs by owner, szlachta in the western provinces. A family missing from one
  tale may be in another of the same year.
- Ages are by eye; read `record-conventions.md` on ages before comparing them.
- The early revisions (1st to 3rd) and the landrat books of the 1710s are largely in the
  Russian State Archive of Ancient Acts (RGADA, f. 350). Later revisions are in the
  provincial archives, and many are on FamilySearch.

## Service people, 16th to early 18th century

For the southern frontier (Kursk, Voronezh, Belgorod lines) the ancestors of odnodvortsy
appear as service people: muster rolls (desyatni), cadastral and census books (pistsovye and
perepisnye knigi), allotment records. Volunteer databases compile them; treat them like any
compiled index. One compiler writes that some documents went in as short extracts only, so
a person missing from the database proves nothing about the documents.

Link a compiled row to a person only when the household proves it: the same father, the
same brothers, the same village across two documents. A name and a village are not enough.

## Metric books and confession lists

- Metric books of Orthodox parishes are in the provincial archives (GA…, TsGA…), copies in
  the consistory series. FamilySearch holds large unindexed collections of them, and a few
  partly indexed Russian Orthodox church book collections; search the collection list for
  "Russia" and the record type.
- A FamilySearch collection is often filed under the wrong province. Find the parish of a
  film through its catalogue entry (the film's creator field), never through the place
  printed on an indexed record, which is often only the district or the empire. Read
  `familysearch-techniques.md`.
- Several Russian archives give online access only through the state services login
  (ESIA). For a researcher without that account the scans are offline; label them
  `[offline]` and look for the FamilySearch copy.

## Lead sources

- **VGD forum** (`forum.vgd.ru`): topics by village, district and surname. Participants post
  extracts and shelfmarks. A post is a lead to the case it cites, never a source.
- Regional volunteer sites index villages, parishes and published lists (for Vyatka province,
  Rodnaya Vyatka at `rodnaya-vyatka.ru`). Use them to find the parish and the case.
- Provincial gazettes (gubernskie vedomosti) and address calendars give clergy, officials
  and landowners by year.
